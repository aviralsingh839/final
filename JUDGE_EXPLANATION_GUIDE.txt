PROJECT TITLE
-------------
CHRONO-PCOS: Multi-Modal Chrono-Metabolic Digital Twin for Educational PCOD/PCOS Risk Estimation

Short title for speaking:
"My project is an offline Arduino + Python biomedical dashboard that estimates PCOD/PCOS risk tendency using multiple physiological signals. It does not diagnose PCOS. It gives an explainable risk score with confidence intervals."

VERY IMPORTANT SAFETY STATEMENT
-------------------------------
This project is NOT a medical diagnostic device.
It does NOT say a person has PCOD or PCOS.
It only estimates a PCOD/PCOS risk tendency using non-invasive physiological signals, manual glucose input, and mathematical/AI models.
Hormones shown on the dashboard are ESTIMATED HORMONE LEVELS, not measured blood or urine hormones.
A real PCOS diagnosis requires a qualified doctor, menstrual history, clinical examination, lab tests, ultrasound/AMH where appropriate, and exclusion of other diseases.

If a judge asks, say:
"I am not diagnosing PCOS. I am building a research-style educational risk-estimation system that shows how sleep, stress, glucose, HRV, circadian rhythm, and hormone-related patterns may contribute to PCOS risk."

============================================================
1. WHY THIS PROJECT IS NEEDED
============================================================

PCOD/PCOS is not caused by only one thing. It is a complex endocrine and metabolic condition. It may involve:

1. Irregular ovulation or irregular menstrual cycles
2. Increased androgen hormones such as testosterone
3. Insulin resistance or metabolic disturbance
4. Sleep and circadian rhythm disturbance
5. Stress and autonomic nervous system imbalance
6. Inflammation and obesity-related risk factors

Many simple school projects try to predict PCOS only from symptoms or one parameter like BMI. My project is different because it uses a multi-signal approach:

- Heart rate
- SpO2 estimate
- PPG waveform
- HRV
- ECG HRV, if AD8232 is used
- Motion/activity
- Skin temperature
- GSR stress signal
- Light exposure
- Environment correction
- Manual glucose
- Optional voice proxy
- Sleep and circadian rhythm analysis

The system combines these into a final explainable PCOD/PCOS Risk Score.

Main innovation:
It creates a "Digital Hormone Twin" and "Chrono-Metabolic Fingerprint" instead of simply asking symptoms.

============================================================
2. WHAT IS PCOD/PCOS IN SIMPLE WORDS?
============================================================

PCOS stands for Polycystic Ovary Syndrome.
PCOD is commonly used in India for Polycystic Ovarian Disease, but medically PCOS is the more accepted term.

In PCOS, the ovaries and hormone system may not work in a regular cyclic pattern. A person may have:

- Irregular periods
- Excess hair growth or acne due to androgens
- Difficulty with ovulation
- Weight gain or insulin resistance
- Higher risk of type 2 diabetes later
- Sleep problems or stress-related symptoms

But not every person has all symptoms. That is why my project uses multiple signals and gives risk, not diagnosis.

============================================================
3. MEDICAL DIAGNOSIS VS MY SYSTEM
============================================================

Clinical PCOS diagnosis generally uses criteria such as:

1. Ovulatory dysfunction / irregular periods
2. Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism, meaning excess androgen signs or blood tests
3. Polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound or AMH in adults, depending on guidelines

Doctors also exclude other causes like thyroid disease, high prolactin, adrenal disease, pregnancy, Cushing syndrome, etc.

My system does not replace this.

My system estimates risk from:

- Metabolic risk
- Sleep risk
- Stress/autonomic risk
- Circadian disruption
- Glucose response
- Estimated hormone tendencies
- Activity and temperature rhythm

So the output is:
"Estimated PCOD/PCOS risk tendency = X%, confidence interval = Y-Z%."

============================================================
4. COMPLETE SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

The project has four main layers:

A. Hardware layer
-----------------
Arduino Mega reads sensors:

- MAX30102: PPG, heart rate, SpO2 estimate, pulse amplitude
- AD8232 ECG: accurate beat-to-beat timing for HRV
- MPU6050: motion and activity
- DS18B20 temperature sensors: skin/fingertip temperature
- GSR: sweat-based stress signal
- BH1750/VEML7700: light exposure for circadian rhythm
- BME280: room temperature, humidity, pressure
- FSR: finger pressure on PPG sensor
- MAX4466 microphone: experimental voice feature
- Glucometer: manual glucose entry

B. Serial communication layer
-----------------------------
Arduino sends sensor data to Python using a serial packet.

Mega packet format:
$CP2,ms,ir,red,ax,ay,az,gx,gy,gz,temp0,temp1,gsr,micRaw,micRms,micPitch,ecg,fsr,lux,roomT,hum,press,buttons,status,crc

This contains all raw sensor values plus status flags.

C. Signal processing layer
--------------------------
Python converts raw sensor signals into biomedical features:

- HR from PPG or ECG
- HRV from beat intervals
- SpO2 estimate from red/IR PPG ratio
- Motion index from accelerometer
- Stress index from HR, HRV, GSR
- Skin temperature trend
- Sleep probability
- Circadian stability
- Metabolic risk

D. AI / mathematical model layer
--------------------------------
The system estimates:

- Insulin resistance probability
- Estimated hormone levels
- Sleep quality
- Circadian rhythm disruption
- Stress/autonomic imbalance
- Final PCOD/PCOS risk score
- Confidence interval
- Explanation of contributing factors

E. Dashboard layer
------------------
The Python PySide6 desktop dashboard shows the results in a premium cyan-black interface.

============================================================
5. WHY ARDUINO MEGA?
============================================================

Initially Arduino UNO can work for basic sensors, but the full project uses Arduino Mega because:

1. More analog pins:
   - GSR on A0
   - Microphone on A1
   - ECG on A2
   - FSR on A3

2. More memory than UNO
   OLED, BME280, MPU6050, MAX30102 libraries together may be heavy for UNO.

3. More digital pins for buttons, LEDs, ECG lead-off pins, buzzer.

4. More stable for premium multi-sensor project.

If a judge asks why not ESP32:
"ESP32 is powerful, but Arduino Mega is easier to explain, easier to wire, and more suitable for a school-level embedded project while still supporting all required sensors."

6. SENSOR-BY-SENSOR EXPLANATION
============================================================

6.1 MAX30102 PPG Sensor
-----------------------
What it measures:
- Red and infrared light reflected from blood in the finger
- Blood volume changes with each heartbeat

Used for:
- Heart rate
- SpO2 estimate
- PPG waveform
- Pulse amplitude
- Metabolic-vascular response index IMVI

How it works:
The sensor shines red and infrared light into the finger. Blood absorbs light differently during each heartbeat. The variation is called PPG or photoplethysmography.

Important features:
- Peak-to-peak pulse waveform gives heart rate.
- Red/IR ratio gives estimated SpO2.
- Pulse amplitude can indicate peripheral blood flow.

Limitation:
PPG is sensitive to finger movement and pressure. That is why I added motion detection and FSR pressure sensor.

6.2 AD8232 ECG Module
---------------------
What it measures:
- Electrical activity of the heart

Used for:
- More accurate heart rate
- More accurate HRV

Why ECG improves accuracy:
PPG detects blood pulse arrival after the heartbeat, while ECG detects the heart electrical beat itself. ECG is better for beat-to-beat timing.

Safety:
- Laptop should be on battery when using ECG electrodes.
- It is only for HRV timing, not ECG diagnosis.

6.3 MPU6050 Accelerometer + Gyroscope
-------------------------------------
What it measures:
- Acceleration in x, y, z directions
- Rotation/gyro movement

Used for:
- Motion index
- Activity level
- Sleep restlessness
- Motion artifact detection

Why it matters:
If heart rate is high but motion is also high, it may be exercise, not stress. The model reduces stress confidence when motion is high.

6.4 DS18B20 Temperature Sensors
-------------------------------
What they measure:
- Skin/fingertip temperature

Used for:
- Skin temperature rhythm
- Sleep/circadian analysis
- Progesterone/ovulation pattern estimate
- Metabolic-vascular temperature response IMTI

Why temperature matters:
After ovulation, progesterone can increase basal body temperature. Also, fingertip warming or cooling can reflect blood flow changes.

Limitation:
Skin temperature is not core temperature. It is affected by room temperature, fan, sweat, and sensor contact.

6.5 GSR Sensor
--------------
What it measures:
- Galvanic skin response / skin conductance
- Sweat-related electrical changes on skin

Used for:
- Sympathetic nervous system activity
- Acute stress
- Chronic stress estimate
- Autonomic response after glucose/meal

Why it matters:
Stress activates the sympathetic nervous system. Sweat gland activity increases, causing skin conductance changes. GSR helps detect stress better than heart rate alone.

6.6 BH1750 or VEML7700 Light Sensor
-----------------------------------
What it measures:
- Light intensity in lux

Used for:
- Circadian rhythm stability
- Day/night light exposure
- Sleep timing interpretation

Why light matters:
Circadian rhythm is controlled strongly by light. Bright light at night can disturb melatonin rhythm and sleep timing. Poor circadian rhythm may worsen metabolic health.

6.7 BME280 Environment Sensor
-----------------------------
What it measures:
- Room temperature
- Humidity
- Atmospheric pressure

Used for:
- Artifact correction
- GSR interpretation
- Skin temperature interpretation

Why environment matters:
If skin temperature drops, it may be due to cold room, not vascular problem. If GSR rises, it may be humidity/sweat, not stress. BME280 helps explain these artifacts.

6.8 FSR Finger Pressure Sensor
------------------------------
What it measures:
- Pressure applied by finger on sensor

Used for:
- Correcting PPG amplitude artifacts

Why it matters:
PPG amplitude changes if the finger presses harder or lighter. The metabolic-vascular index IMVI uses PPG amplitude, so finger pressure must be monitored.

6.9 MAX4466 Microphone / VoxVasc
--------------------------------
What it measures:
- Voice sound amplitude
- Approximate pitch frequency

Used for:
- Experimental VoxVasc voice proxy

Important explanation:
Voice is NOT a testosterone test. Some studies show hormones can influence voice pitch over long periods, but individual prediction is weak. My project keeps this module low-confidence and low-weight.

If judge asks:
"Why include voice if it is weak?"
Answer:
"I included it as an experimental module to show multi-modal biomarker fusion, but I clearly label it low confidence and do not use it as a major diagnostic feature."

6.10 Glucometer
---------------
What it measures:
- Blood glucose manually entered into dashboard

Used for:
- Glucose risk
- Insulin resistance probability
- Metabolic challenge response

Why glucose matters:
Insulin resistance is common in PCOS. If glucose remains high after meal/challenge, it may suggest reduced glucose clearance. But glucose alone cannot diagnose PCOS.

8. GLUCOSE EXPLANATION IN DETAIL
============================================================

What is glucose?
----------------
Glucose is the main sugar in blood. It comes from food, especially carbohydrates.

What is insulin?
----------------
Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas. It helps cells take glucose from the blood.

What is insulin resistance?
---------------------------
Insulin resistance means cells do not respond properly to insulin. The pancreas may produce more insulin to compensate. This can cause hyperinsulinemia, meaning high insulin levels.

Why insulin resistance matters in PCOS:
--------------------------------------
In many PCOS cases, insulin resistance can increase androgen production in ovaries and reduce SHBG from the liver. Lower SHBG means more free testosterone is available. This can worsen acne, hair growth, and ovulatory problems.

Dashboard glucose fields:
-------------------------
The dashboard asks for glucose manually because the Arduino does not directly read glucometer strips.

Glucose contexts:

1. Fasting glucose
   Measured after no food for 8-12 hours.

2. 2-hour post-meal glucose
   Measured about 2 hours after eating.

3. Random glucose
   Measured anytime, harder to interpret.

How glucose risk is calculated:
-------------------------------
For fasting glucose:
- Around 70-99 mg/dL is usually considered normal in many clinical references.
- Higher values increase metabolic risk score.

For 2-hour post-meal glucose:
- Lower values indicate better glucose clearance.
- Higher values suggest impaired glucose handling.

But my project does not diagnose diabetes. It only uses glucose as one risk feature.

If judge asks:
"Can glucose alone detect PCOS?"
Answer:
"No. Glucose alone cannot detect PCOS. It only helps estimate metabolic risk. PCOS is endocrine-metabolic, so glucose is one piece of the multi-modal model."

9. HORMONES EXPLAINED
============================================================

9.1 Insulin
-----------
Source:
Pancreas

Main function:
Moves glucose from blood into cells.

Relation to PCOS:
Insulin resistance can lead to high insulin. High insulin may stimulate ovarian androgen production and lower SHBG, increasing free androgen effect.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Manual glucose
- BMI/waist if entered
- Sleep risk
- Stress
- Activity
- Circadian rhythm

Confidence:
Medium if recent glucose is available, lower if glucose is missing.

9.2 Testosterone
----------------
Source:
Ovaries and adrenal glands in females, in smaller amounts than males.

Main function:
An androgen hormone. Normal female bodies have testosterone, but excess can cause acne, hirsutism, and irregular cycles.

Relation to PCOS:
Hyperandrogenism is one of the key PCOS features.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Insulin resistance probability
- LH/FSH tendency
- BMI
- Sleep/circadian stress
- PCOS latent risk pattern

Important:
It is NOT measured. Real testosterone requires blood test.

If judge asks:
"How can you show testosterone without a blood test?"
Answer:
"I do not measure it. I estimate a testosterone tendency using known physiological relationships, and I show a wide confidence interval. The dashboard clearly says estimated hormone level, not measured hormone level."

9.3 LH - Luteinizing Hormone
----------------------------
Full form:
Luteinizing Hormone

Source:
Pituitary gland in the brain

Main function:
LH helps trigger ovulation. It also stimulates ovarian theca cells, which can produce androgens.

Relation to PCOS:
Some PCOS patients have higher LH or higher LH/FSH ratio, but this is not present in everyone and is not required for diagnosis.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Cycle phase
- Stress/circadian disruption
- PCOS latent tendency
- Estimated progesterone/ovulation pattern

Important:
LH is pulsatile. It changes throughout the day and cycle. Real LH requires blood/urine test.

9.4 FSH - Follicle Stimulating Hormone
--------------------------------------
Full form:
Follicle Stimulating Hormone

Source:
Pituitary gland

Main function:
FSH helps ovarian follicles grow and mature.

Relation to PCOS:
In some PCOS patterns, FSH may be relatively lower compared to LH, contributing to poor follicle maturation. But this is not universal.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Cycle phase
- Age
- PCOS latent tendency

If judge asks:
"Why LH/FSH ratio?"
Answer:
"LH/FSH ratio is an old supportive endocrine pattern seen in some PCOS cases. It is not diagnostic by itself. I use it only as a weak estimated endocrine feature, not as a final diagnosis."

9.5 Estrogen / Estradiol
------------------------
Source:
Growing ovarian follicles and other tissues

Main function:
Supports menstrual cycle, uterine lining growth, bone health, and reproductive function.

Cycle pattern:
Estrogen rises before ovulation and may rise again in the luteal phase.

Relation to PCOS:
In irregular/anovulatory cycles, estrogen rhythm may not follow a normal cyclic pattern.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Cycle day
- Temperature rhythm
- BMI
- Circadian disruption

Important:
Real estrogen requires blood test. My dashboard estimates only a tendency.

9.6 Progesterone
----------------
Source:
Corpus luteum after ovulation

Main function:
Supports luteal phase and prepares uterus after ovulation.

Relation to temperature:
Progesterone has a thermogenic effect. After ovulation, basal temperature often rises slightly.

Relation to PCOS:
If ovulation does not occur regularly, progesterone may be low in the luteal phase. This can suggest an anovulatory pattern.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Cycle phase
- Skin/basal temperature rise
- Sleep/stress/circadian pattern

Important:
Temperature is only an indirect clue. Real progesterone requires blood test.

9.7 Cortisol
------------
Source:
Adrenal glands

Main function:
Stress hormone. It follows a circadian rhythm: normally higher in morning and lower at night.

Relation to PCOS risk:
Chronic stress and poor sleep may increase cortisol load, worsen insulin resistance, and disturb reproductive hormone rhythm.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Time of day
- HRV
- GSR
- Sleep disruption
- Circadian disruption

Important:
Cortisol is estimated as a trend, not a lab value.

9.8 AMH - Anti-Mullerian Hormone
--------------------------------
Full form:
Anti-Mullerian Hormone

Source:
Small ovarian follicles

Main function:
Marker related to ovarian follicle pool.

Relation to PCOS:
AMH can be higher in PCOS because there may be more small follicles. Recent guidelines allow AMH to support diagnosis in adults in some situations, but not in adolescents in the same way.

Dashboard estimate uses:
- Age
- Cycle irregularity
- PCOS latent tendency
- Androgen tendency

Important:
AMH is NOT a real-time hormone. It changes slowly. My dashboard shows AMH tendency with very low confidence unless lab data is entered.

If judge asks:
"Can your system measure AMH?"
Answer:
"No. AMH requires a lab test. My dashboard only estimates AMH tendency based on public hormone priors and PCOS-related patterns. It is clearly labelled low confidence."

10. WHAT IS DIGITAL HORMONE TWIN?
============================================================

A digital twin means a computer model that represents a biological system.

My Digital Hormone Twin does this:

Current physiology
heart rate, HRV, GSR, glucose, sleep, temperature, activity
        ↓
Metabolic state
insulin resistance probability, glucose risk
        ↓
Endocrine tendency
estimated insulin, cortisol, testosterone, LH, FSH, estrogen, progesterone, AMH
        ↓
PCOD/PCOS risk estimate

It is called a twin because it simulates how the user's physiology may relate to endocrine patterns.

But it is not a real hormone laboratory.

11. SLEEP ANALYSIS EXPLANATION
============================================================

Why sleep matters:
Poor sleep can increase stress hormones, worsen insulin sensitivity, increase cravings, and disturb reproductive hormone rhythm.

Sensors used:
- Heart rate
- HRV
- Motion
- Skin temperature
- GSR
- Time of day
- SpO2 estimate

Sleep probability increases when:
- Motion is low
- HR is lower than daytime
- HRV is higher
- Temperature is stable
- Time is night
- GSR stress is low

Deep sleep estimate:
Higher when:
- Motion is very low
- HR is low
- RMSSD HRV is higher
- It is early night

REM probability:
Estimated from:
- Later night timing
- HRV variability
- Low motion

Important:
This is not EEG sleep staging. Real sleep staging needs EEG/EOG/EMG. My project gives approximate sleep quality, not clinical sleep diagnosis.

12. CIRCADIAN RHYTHM EXPLANATION
============================================================

Circadian rhythm is the body's 24-hour biological clock.

It controls:
- Sleep/wake timing
- Cortisol rhythm
- Melatonin rhythm
- Body temperature rhythm
- Glucose metabolism
- Hormone timing

My dashboard calculates Circadian Stability Index using:

1. Heart rate rhythm
2. Temperature rhythm
3. Activity rhythm
4. Sleep timing
5. Light exposure

A stable rhythm means:
- Regular sleep/wake timing
- Bright light during day
- Low light at night
- Predictable activity/rest pattern

Circadian disruption may increase PCOS-related risk because it can worsen insulin resistance and stress hormone rhythm.

============================================================
13. STRESS AND HRV EXPLANATION
============================================================

Heart Rate Variability, HRV:
HRV means variation in time between heartbeats.

Example:
If heartbeats are exactly equally spaced, HRV is low.
If intervals vary naturally, HRV is higher.

RMSSD:
RMSSD is a time-domain HRV measure. It reflects parasympathetic/vagal activity.

Stress effect:
During stress:
- Heart rate often increases
- RMSSD often decreases
- GSR often increases
- Skin temperature may change

My stress index combines:
- HR increase
- RMSSD decrease
- GSR increase
- Motion correction
- Temperature trend

Why motion correction matters:
Exercise also increases HR. If motion is high, the dashboard reduces stress confidence.

============================================================
14. METABOLIC-VASCULAR CHALLENGE / MV-AST EXPLANATION
============================================================

This module is inspired by the concept that insulin affects blood vessels.

In a healthy response:
After food/carbohydrate, insulin helps glucose uptake and may support nitric oxide-mediated vasodilation. Peripheral blood flow may increase.

In insulin resistance:
The vasodilatory response may be weaker. But this is complex and cannot be used alone for diagnosis.

My project measures dynamic response:

Baseline state:
- glucose
- PPG amplitude
- temperature
- autonomic load

Post-meal/challenge state:
- glucose
- PPG amplitude
- temperature
- autonomic load

Indices:

Delta G:
Delta G = post glucose - baseline glucose
Shows glucose rise.

IMVI:
IMVI = post PPG amplitude / baseline PPG amplitude
Shows pulse amplitude response.

IMTI:
IMTI = post temperature - baseline temperature
Shows thermal/perfusion response.

ARR:
ARR = post autonomic load / baseline autonomic load
Shows whether stress/autonomic load increased after meal.

Important:
PPG amplitude is affected by finger pressure, motion, temperature, caffeine, and anxiety. That is why I added FSR pressure and BME280 environment monitoring.

============================================================
16. CONFIDENCE INTERVAL EXPLANATION
============================================================

A confidence interval shows uncertainty.

Example:
Risk = 42%
90% CI = 31% to 56%

Meaning:
Because sensors and hormone estimates are uncertain, the model does not pretend the risk is exactly 42%. It says the likely range is 31-56%.

Why confidence may be low:
- Poor PPG contact
- High motion
- No glucose entered
- No sleep data
- No personal baseline
- Hormones are estimated, not measured
- ECG leads off
- GSR not connected

If judge asks:
"Why show confidence interval?"
Answer:
"A responsible biomedical AI system must show uncertainty. Without uncertainty, the result may look more accurate than it actually is."

19. LIMITATIONS TO HONESTLY TELL JUDGES
============================================================

A strong science project should admit limitations.

Limitations:

1. It cannot diagnose PCOS.
2. Hormones are estimated, not measured.
3. SpO2 from MAX30102 is educational unless calibrated.
4. PPG is sensitive to motion and finger pressure.
5. Skin temperature is affected by environment.
6. Sleep staging without EEG is approximate.
7. Voice biomarkers are experimental and low confidence.
8. AMH cannot be estimated accurately in real time.
9. Manual glucose depends on correct user entry and meal context.
10. Public datasets may not perfectly match Indian adolescents.
11. Clinical validation with doctors would be needed before any healthcare use.

If judge asks:

Answer:
"The biggest limitation is that reproductive hormones cannot be truly measured non-invasively by these sensors. That is why I show them as estimated with confidence intervals and keep the system as risk estimation only."

21. COMMON JUDGE QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
============================================================

Q1. Does your project diagnose PCOS?
A. No. It estimates risk tendency only. Diagnosis requires a doctor, clinical history, lab tests, and exclusion of other disorders.

Q2. How can sensors estimate hormones?
A. They cannot measure hormones directly. I use physiological relationships and public database priors to estimate hormone tendencies with uncertainty. The dashboard clearly says estimated, not measured.

Q3. Why include glucose?
A. Insulin resistance is common in PCOS. Glucose helps estimate metabolic risk and insulin resistance probability, but it is only one part of the model.

Q4. What are LH and FSH?
A. They are pituitary hormones. LH helps trigger ovulation and androgen production. FSH helps follicles mature. Some PCOS patients have altered LH/FSH pattern, but it is not diagnostic by itself.

Q5. What is AMH?
A. AMH is Anti-Mullerian Hormone from small ovarian follicles. It can be higher in PCOS, but it requires lab testing. My system estimates only AMH tendency with low confidence.

Q6. Why use HRV?
A. HRV shows autonomic nervous system balance. Low RMSSD often indicates stress or reduced parasympathetic activity, which can affect sleep and metabolic health.

Q7. Why use GSR?
A. GSR reflects sweat gland activity controlled by sympathetic nervous system. It helps detect stress beyond heart rate alone.

Q8. Why use circadian rhythm?
A. Circadian rhythm affects cortisol, melatonin, sleep, glucose metabolism, and reproductive hormones. Disruption can worsen metabolic risk.

Q9. What makes your AI explainable?
A. The dashboard shows domain scores and top contributing factors, such as glucose risk, sleep disruption, stress, or endocrine tendency. It does not just output a black-box result.

Q10. Why use Arduino Mega?
A. Mega has more analog pins and memory, allowing ECG, GSR, microphone, FSR, OLED, and multiple I2C sensors together.

Q11. What is VoxVasc?
A. VoxVasc is an experimental voice biomarker module. It analyzes pitch and voice energy but is low confidence and not a hormone test.

Q12. What is MV-AST?
A. MV-AST is Metabolic-Vascular-Autonomic Stress Test. It compares baseline and post-meal glucose, PPG amplitude, temperature, and autonomic response.

Q13. Why not use cloud AI?
A. The project is fully offline for privacy, reliability, and exhibition use.

Q14. What if the risk is high?
A. The dashboard says the person may consider professional evaluation if symptomatic. It does not diagnose or prescribe treatment.

Q15. How do you validate it?
A. Sensors can be compared with reference devices. Stress model can be trained on WESAD. Sleep model can be validated on PhysioNet/MESA. Hormone priors can be generated from NHANES/mcPHASES. Final clinical validation would require doctor-supervised studies.
